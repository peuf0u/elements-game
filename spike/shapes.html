Elements · design sketch

Piece shapes

Each element gets a shape that hints at how it moves, so you can read the board without relying on colour. Colour stays as a second cue. The dots still show size, and the owner still shows as the light or dark rim.

One shape per element

Combined pieces: three ways

Only three pairs really move two ways: Fire + Wind, Fire + Water and Wind + Water. A stack moves at its larger size, and it never splits again, so it only needs one set of dots: its range. Each example is a size 3 stacked with a size 1.

Earth joining an attacker is different. The stack moves only as the attacker, so it keeps the attacker's shape, with a small brown octagon in the middle to show the Earth inside. It looks the same whichever option you pick:

My pick

A · Merged

  • The outline is exactly how the stack moves. Fire + Wind's square and diamond together make an eight-point star, and it moves in all eight directions (one kind per move).
  • A stack has a different outline from every single piece, so you can spot stacks across the board, which matters because taking one wins two elements.
  • Earth stacks keep the attacker's outline, which matches the rule that they move only as the attacker.
  • The weak point: Fire + Water and Wind + Water are less striking than Fire + Wind, and on a black piece the colours carry more of the load.
